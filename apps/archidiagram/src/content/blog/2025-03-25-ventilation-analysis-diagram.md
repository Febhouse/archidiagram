# Tutorial on how to create a ventilation analysis diagram

Want to show how air flows through your building? Here’s a quick, step-by-step guide on creating a clean **ventilation analysis diagram** using SketchUp, RWIND, and Photoshop.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE

## Applicable for:

1. Ventilation diagrams
2. Wind flow analysis
3. Indoor environmental studies
4. Architectural diagram

## Softwares used:

1. **SketchUp**: For basic 3D modeling.
2. **RWIND Simulation**: For calculating the airflow physics (the free trial version works fine!).
3. **Photoshop**: To put everything together and make it look great.

## **STEP 1: Create a 3D model in SketchUp**

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE&t=12s

1. Open SketchUp and model your room/site.
2. **Keep it simple:** Only model the essential blockers like walls. Use simplified shapes for trees (e.g., a sphere on a cylinder).
3. Set your units to **Meters** (`Window > Model Info > Units`).
4. Export the model as an **STL file** (`File > Export > 3D Model`).

<a id="step2"></a>
## **STEP 2: Import the Model into Rwind Simulation**

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE&t=92s

1. Open RWIND Simulation and create a new project.
2. Import your `.stl` file (make sure units are still set to meters).
3. Shrink the **simulation box** down to just cover your area of interest. This drastically reduces calculation time!

<a id="step3"></a>
## **STEP 3: Set Up the Simulation in Rwind**

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE&t=184s

1. Go to **Edit Simulation** to tweak the physics.
2. Set your **inlet wind speed** (e.g., 5 m/s).
3. Drop the **mesh density** to around 20%—this gives a great balance between speed and accuracy.
4. Click **Calculate Results** and grab a coffee while it processes.

<a id="step4"></a>
## **STEP 4: Visualizing Airflow**

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE&t=241s

1. Go to **Flow Field Quantities** and select the **XY plane**.
2. For the best visual result, choose the **Streamline** view (this creates those nice continuous airflow paths).
3. Tweak the streamline density and size until it looks right.
4. Switch to a **top-down (plan) view**, and turn off the background/grid textures.
5. Click **Start > Flow Animation**, and export a 10-second video of the airflow.

<a id="step5"></a>
## **STEP 5:Post-Processing in Photoshop**

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ir4KS6us0HE&t=380s

1. Open Photoshop and drag in your RWIND video.
2. Turn on the **Timeline** panel.
3. Drop in the PNG images (furniture, trees, textures) over the video.
4. Draw crisp outlines over your walls to make the architecture read clearly.
5. Extend all your image layers on the timeline so they match the video's length.
6. Render the final video.
